Where parents actually look
The decision journey

The decision is made in about two weeks, mostly on a phone.

A parent does not compare twenty schools. They compare three, on a handset, at night, with a spouse looking over their shoulder. Each stage below has one thing the school must own — and most schools own the wrong one.

01
Family · neighbour · group

A name comes up

A relative mentions a school, a board result is discussed, a transfer lands. The parent asks two people they trust.

Your only asset here: the one sentence a parent repeats about you.
02
9pm, phone in hand

They search

“school near me”, “CBSE school {{CITY}}”, “school fees”. They scroll the map and the first screen of Google.

Yours: the listing, the photos, the reviews, the page that loads in two seconds.
03
Three to five names

They shortlist

Transport, board, fee band, class size, safety. Usually compared over a screenshot sent to a spouse.

Yours: those five answers, above the fold, in writing.
04
The quiet check

They verify

They read reviews, open Instagram, message the school, and ask a parent who already sends there.

Yours: reviews replied to, real photographs, a human answering WhatsApp.
05
The campus

They visit

They walk the corridor, meet a teacher, see a classroom. The decision usually lands within a day of the visit.

Yours: one-tap booking, a named host, same-day follow-up.
Three moments we engineer, because we cannot control the rest
1
The 9pm search

Ranking in the Map pack for “school near me” is not vanity — it is the first shortlist. It is won with a complete listing, photographs and reviews, not with an advertisement.

2
The first reply

A parent messages three schools. The one that replies first, in a human voice, with a visit slot, usually gets the visit. Target: five minutes in school hours.

3
The campus visit

The cheapest admission is the one who is already standing in your corridor. Everything after the visit is follow-up, not persuasion.

Why this changes the plan

Most school marketing budgets go to the stage parents trust least — advertising. We spend first on the three stages parents actually use: map, message, visit.

Pillar 01 · Be found
Pillar 01 · Be found

Own the map, the search bar, and the answer an assistant gives.

Three surfaces decide whether you are in the shortlist: the map pack, the first screen of Google, and — increasingly — the reply a parent gets when they ask an AI assistant which schools exist in {{CITY}}. All three are fed by the same facts, so all three improve together.

The Google listing — your busiest shopfront

Complete it once, then keep it alive. This is the single highest-return asset a local school owns.

  • Claim and verify; primary category School, secondary categories sensible.
  • Twenty or more real photographs: campus, classrooms, labs, library, transport, staff.
  • Correct hours, admission-season note, and a booking link to the admissions page.
  • One post a week in season — results, events, admissions dates, a teacher profile.
  • Seed the questions parents actually ask, and answer them yourself.
  • Review link in every parent WhatsApp; reply to all reviews within 48 hours.
  • Track calls and direction requests with UTM links, not guesswork.

The admissions page — one screen, five answers

A visitor decides in eight seconds whether to stay. Give them the five facts before asking for anything.

  • Board and medium, grades offered, and the admission year in the headline.
  • Fee band in writing — “from ₹— per year” beats “contact us for fees”.
  • Admission dates and the exact process, in three steps.
  • Transport routes by town, with the nearest stop named.
  • Proof: results as published, teachers, safety, photographs with consent.
  • Three-field form, click-to-call, click-to-WhatsApp, sticky on mobile.

Paid traffic sent to an incomplete listing and a slow page is a donation to Google. Fixing the surface first means every rupee of media lands on something that can convert — and organic visibility keeps working in the months you do not spend.

The review engine — three steps, run every term
1
Ask at the moment of gratitude

Report day, annual day, a solved transport problem, a parent-teacher meeting that went well. One WhatsApp, one direct review link, one sentence of context — no incentive, ever.

2
Reply to everything within 48 hours

Thank the specific thing. On a negative review: acknowledge, correct the fact if it is wrong, take the conversation offline, never argue in public.

3
Put the best proof where the decision happens

Three reviews on the admissions page, one in the ad creative, one in the follow-up message — matched to the worry it answers.

Consent is not paperwork

A child’s photograph and name are personal data under the DPDP Act 2023. We keep a signed, specific, withdrawable parental consent for every child, know where each image is used, and take it down within 48 hours of a withdrawal.

Claims we will not make

No “number one school”, no “100% results”, no guaranteed rank or seat. Education advertising in India is judged on substantiation — an unverifiable superlative is a liability, not a slogan.

New reviews from current parents — the honest scoreboard, not a bought one.

Pillar 04 · Be converted
Pillar 04 · Be converted

The funnel is a phone call, not a form.

In this market the enquiry arrives on WhatsApp, usually after 8pm, usually while the parent is comparing three schools. The school that answers like a human, within minutes, with a visit slot, gets the family. Everything below makes that reliable.

The admissions page, top to bottom
1
Promise, in one line

Board, grades, town and admission year — legible before the first scroll, on a 5-inch screen.

2
Proof, immediately after

Three reviews, one published result, one teacher, one photograph of a real classroom.

3
Practicalities, in a table

Fee band, transport routes, timings, documents. Tables get screenshotted and forwarded — that is the point.

4
A person, not a logo

Name, face and direct number of the admissions coordinator. Parents call a person.

5
One action, repeated

Book a campus visit. Not “enquire”, not “download brochure”. A visit has a date, and a date is a decision.

WhatsApp, set up properly

  • Business profile with hours, address and catalogue of six answers.
  • Greeting message outside hours that still books a slot.
  • Six saved replies: fees, transport, documents, timings, results, visit.
  • Labels for every stage so nothing is remembered by memory.
  • One named owner, plus one backup — never a shared inbox with no owner.

The five-minute rule

First reply inside five minutes during school hours. Outside them, an honest auto-reply with two visit slots. Speed is the cheapest conversion improvement available to a school.

We do not chase, and we never message a number that did not ask.

How the numbers turn into seats

Seats = enquiries × visit rate × admission rate. Worked example with round numbers: 300 enquiries × 40% visit × 35% admission = 42 seats. We replace all three numbers with your measured baseline — the arithmetic is the point, not the example.

Sankranti — 14–17 January

The single biggest attention window in coastal Andhra. Families gather, decisions are discussed. We publish the invitation a week early and staff the WhatsApp line through the holiday.

Results day — April

The one moment your proof is undeniable and every competitor is quiet or boasting. Published results, named support, no exaggeration — and a live page ready before the board uploads.

Fee deadline — May

Honest urgency: a real date, a real seat count. A countdown to a deadline that does not exist is the fastest way to lose a parent’s trust at the last step.

The rule for the whole calendar

Everything is published at least a week before it is needed. A parent who learns about your open house on the morning of it was never going to attend — and the school that tells them early looks organised, which is itself a selling point.

The rules we work under

DPDP Act 2023

Children’s data needs verifiable parental consent, a stated purpose and a retention limit. Photographs, names and contact numbers are personal data, not marketing material.

ASCI education code

No guaranteed ranks, marks or seats. Every comparison and superlative must be substantiated. No disparagement of a named institution, and no misleading fee claims.

Board name and logo

Affiliation is stated as a fact, not used as an endorsement. Logos appear only as the board permits, and the affiliation number is shown where required.

Review integrity

No incentives, no staff-written reviews, no gating. Ask every parent, publish every reply. A 4.4 with 120 real reviews is worth more than a perfect fake.

WhatsApp policy

Message only people who asked to hear from you. No purchased lists, no bulk blasts, an easy opt-out on every sequence.

Accessibility

Readable contrast, real text instead of text-in-images, labelled form fields, 44 px tap targets. Parents on a three-year-old phone are the majority, not the edge case.

Our red lines — the work stops rather than crosses these

No bought contact lists. No fake or incentivised reviews. No guaranteed rank, result or seat. No photograph of a child without a signed consent. No public disparagement of a named competitor. No fee claim that is not in writing. If a request needs one of these to succeed, the request is the problem.
